What fertilizing and weed control do

Fertilizing supplies nutrients to support grass growth when the lawn needs them. Weed control focuses on reducing unwanted plants in turf. These are different tasks, and an appropriate plan depends on the grass type, growing conditions, existing weeds, and the goals for the property.

Understand the lawn first

Thin grass, discoloration, or weeds can have different causes. Sharing what you see helps guide a discussion about whether fertilizing, weed control, or another type of care may be appropriate.

Plan for timing

Grass and weeds respond differently across the year. Timing and the condition of the lawn matter, so a service plan should be based on the property rather than a one-size-fits-all schedule.

Discuss the service scope

Tell us which areas need attention and any concerns about plants, pets, or use of the yard so the requested work can be discussed clearly.

Why a lawn may need nutrient or weed care

When grass lacks appropriate nutrients or weeds spread through the turf, the lawn can look thin or uneven and may be harder to maintain. Addressing the underlying conditions can be part of a broader lawn-care plan. No treatment can be chosen responsibly without considering the lawn and its particular needs.
