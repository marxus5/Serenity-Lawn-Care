What sod installation and seeding do

Sod is mature grass with a layer of soil that is installed over a prepared area, providing immediate ground cover. Seeding places grass seed on prepared soil and relies on germination and establishment over time. Both methods can create a lawn, but they have different timelines and preparation and care needs.

Sod for immediate coverage

Sod can cover bare ground as soon as it is installed. It needs proper soil preparation and consistent aftercare while the grass roots establish in its new location.

Seed for gradual growth

Seeding can establish grass in a new area or help refresh a lawn. Germination and coverage take time and depend on suitable seed, conditions, and care.

Prepare the site

Existing vegetation, grading, soil conditions, access, and the area size all affect the scope. Discuss the site before deciding which approach fits.

Why a yard may need new sod or seed

Bare patches, damaged turf, or a newly created lawn area can leave soil exposed and disrupt the look and use of a yard. Installing sod or seeding can establish grass coverage, but the best choice depends on timing, desired speed, site conditions, and the care available after installation.
