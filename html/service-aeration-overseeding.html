All lawn and yard services / Aeration and overseeding

What aeration and overseeding do

Aeration creates openings in the soil to help air, water, and nutrients reach the root zone. Overseeding spreads grass seed across an existing lawn to encourage new grass growth. These services are often considered together, but whether they suit a yard depends on its existing grass and growing conditions.

Address compacted soil

Soil compaction can make it more difficult for water and air to move into the root zone. Aeration is used to open the soil and can be considered where compaction is a concern.

Add seed to thin turf

Overseeding introduces seed to an established lawn where grass coverage has become sparse. Seed type, timing, and aftercare all affect how well new grass can establish.

Consider the lawn as a whole

Thin turf can have multiple causes. A discussion of the yard's current condition helps determine whether aeration, overseeding, or a different service fits your goals.

When to consider lawn aeration or overseeding

Compacted areas, heavy foot traffic, or grass that has become thin may be reasons to ask about these services. Aeration and overseeding are not instant fixes: the right timing, suitable seed, and follow-up care are important, and results vary with conditions.
